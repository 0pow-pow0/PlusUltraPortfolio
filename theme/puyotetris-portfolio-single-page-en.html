    🔧 The Grid - Methodology of thought and resolution problems

    🧭 CONTEXT!

    Creating the grid was certainly the most complicated part, coordinates, coordinate conversions, index conversions and so on, this was the most difficult challenge, as a simple mistake meant a overflow error or incorrect coordinates of the pieces and let's remember that not there is a debugger for bad math calculations xD.

    At the beginning the most delicate problem was making the world of Unity coexist with the chessboard. In Tetris the pieces fall, the lower you are, the more your index increases, for example, a newly spawned piece will have Y = 0 while a piece that has reached the end of the grid will be Y = (Grid height). In Unity, on the other hand, works the other way around. I therefore had to avoid a choice of coordinates would make already fixed walls, bottom and blocks ambiguous.

    Then, a piece had to be spawned via unity's World Coordinates, but converted into indices to verify its position within the grid, wait... now that piece has to move, then we have to update the index and move within the grid, then update the our World Coordinates based on the size of each block of the grill. What if the grid moved within the Unity scene? The transformation of the tetromino had to also include the position of the grid in the world.

    The solution to these problems was relatively simple in reality, it only required good use of the fundamental mathematical operators, the part It was difficult to deeply understand the logical challenge faced and the solutions, without ever losing the thread and without drooling anywhere, too because a plus instead of a minus would have completely messed up a calculation, forcing me to review everything.

    In addition to the logical part, I will focus on the method I have used to solve this problem.

    First of all I took pen and paper and made a drawing that represented a real gameplay situation, to understand which ones challenges I would have faced before even having to touch the code.

    ❓ WHY?

    The drawing allowed me to fully understand the logical challenges involved when the grid was built, there was no question of any problems here architectural aspects of the software, but strictly dependent logic problems from the context they were part of. This way I had a map that could consistently answer my questions and was able to explain the problem even to a stranger, losing track of it was impossible and was assured that I had complete vision and understanding of the problem in all its forms.

    (Idea of the drawing I had done, AI GENERATED)

    Once you understand the problem, the necessary parameters and the logical challenges to face, I tried my hand at making it, which included how first step, the creation of the grid. Obviously, tetris logic think in cells and grids, not in pixels or realistic physics, therefore, I have exploited the logic of arrays and a logic based on cells and not on transformations

    🛠️ HOW?

    TetrisLogic maintains two two-dimensional arrays with indices [row, column]. GridMapValues[,] preserves the type of a grid tile, the type is described by the enumeration GridMapValues, as explained here. Le tile types may vary between the following:

    • Empty.
    • Wall.
    • Tetramino.
    • Invalid. (This is useful when trying to read an index outside of the grid, very useful for GameOver condition and other edges cases).

    In parallel GameObject[,] retains, however, the reference to the GameObjects of the tetrominoes placed on the map, facilitating the process deleting and reading blocks. This double representation separates the question: "What occupies the cell?", from the question: "Which one object should I animate, move or destroy?".

    GridSquareBottomLeftToWorldCoords and GridSquareCenterToWorldCoords transform the indices into world coordinates starting from gridOrigin and tile size of the grid, which are specified via the Inspector. The functions inverse, as can be imagined, transform any coordinate into an index of the grid, taking into account the fact that, in the chosen model, the Y increases downwards.

int WorldCoordsToGridY(float y)
{
    float accordingToGridOriginY = y - gridOrigin.transform.position.y;
    if (y <= gridOrigin.transform.position.y)
        return Math.Abs((int)accordingToGridOriginY);
    else
        return -((int)accordingToGridOriginY);
}

    ❓ WHY?

    Why is the grid developed in this way?

    I chose a logical grid because a block game must reason in cells, not pixels. It is the most reliable way to know if a tetromino can enter a space, if it has bottomed out and if a line is complete. The transform alone is not a secure basis for rules: it depends on transformations, pivots and roundings.

    🎫 Event-driven design again...

    This game was produced in parallel with Rumble Smash, therefore, many patterns/architectures used in RS are also present here, like this one here!. Let's see why I used it.

    🧭 CONTEXT!

    As in any game, a single action, for example me placing a piece, can generate a chain of events, audio, particles, animations, UI updates or gameplay logic updates. Every event that requires multiple services with single responsibilities becomes one UnityEvent.

    ❓ WHY?

    Why did I use an event-driven approach? What are the advantages?

    By exploiting event-driven design I obtain advantages such as: optimize the use of resources to the bare minimum, avoiding completely the pooling; avoid circular dependencies between scripts, reducing the complexity; increase the testability, the maintainability, the reusability and flexibility of the code. In this project it was particularly useful for managing the many animations parametric, therefore dependent on many lines of code, manage them without single-responsibility would have been painful.

    🛠️ HOW?

    TetrisLogic publishes UnityEvent for motion, rotation, pose, deletion of lines, piece creation, start of the game and game over. The listeners are connected in the Tetris scene: FieldAudioManager plays the clip, FieldAnimationManager animates blocks, UIInGame updates score and preview, UIGameOver shows or hides the panel. The onLineClear event also carries block list, duration and number of lines, sufficient data so that the recipients react without directly questioning the grid.

    In this project, due to the speed of development, I have not performed SOLID approaches, for example, note how "TetrisLogic" contains mixed logic, both of individual tetrominoes and of the state of the match, clearly this is a lesson to be learned for the post-mortem, even though I was perfectly aware of it during creation, but due to the rush, I chose the simplest approach. In any case, the logic event-driven persists and is applied efficiently.

    🤳 Controls for mobile and desktop

    🧭 CONTEXT!

    The first obstacle was the need to have multiple different types of controls touch I had to interpret a continuous movement of the finger and transform it into a in-game action. If I react to every slight change, the piece moves error; if I require gestures that are too long, the game feels slow. I have then searched for a clear boundary between involuntary gesture, directional swipe and prolonged pressure, in this way the pace of the game would not have been possible break.

    🛠️ HOW?

    I created a structure called "TouchManager", it manages the recognition of the various types of touches made by the user. At the end of with a touch the initial and final positions are compared, if they are found below a threshold, it is not recognized as a swipe. Then, comparing the absolute value of the two components of the delta decides whether the gesture is horizontal or vertical and obtains one of the four directions.

    void Update()
    {
        thisFrameTouchInput = DetectedTouchInput.NONE;

        if (Touch.activeTouches.Count > 0)
        {
            Debug.Log("Touches: " + Touch.activeTouches.Count);
            Touch touch = Touch.activeTouches[0];

            switch (touch.phase)
            {
                case UnityEngine.InputSystem.TouchPhase.Began:
                    startTouchPosition = touch.screenPosition;
                    break;

                case UnityEngine.InputSystem.TouchPhase.Stationary:
                    stillTimeTimer += Time.deltaTime;
                    if(stillTimeTimer >= stillTimeTolerance)
                    {
                        thisFrameTouchInput = DetectedTouchInput.LONG_STILL_TOUCH;
                    }
                    break;

                case UnityEngine.InputSystem.TouchPhase.Ended:
                    endTouchPosition = touch.screenPosition;
                    DetectSwipe();
                    break;
            }
        }
    }
    
    void DetectSwipe()
    {
        Vector2 delta = endTouchPosition - startTouchPosition;

        // ? --- Ignora swipe con spostamento minimo
        if (delta.magnitude < swipeTolerance)
            return;

        // ? --- Orizzontale
        if (Mathf.Abs(delta.x) > Mathf.Abs(delta.y))
        {
            if (delta.x > 0)
            {
                thisFrameTouchInput = DetectedTouchInput.SWIPE_RIGHT;
                Debug.Log("Swipe Destra");
            }
            else
            {
                thisFrameTouchInput = DetectedTouchInput.SWIPE_LEFT;
                Debug.Log("Swipe Sinistra");
            }
        }
        // ? --- Verticale
        else
        {
            if (delta.y > 0)
            {
                thisFrameTouchInput = DetectedTouchInput.SWIPE_UP;
                Debug.Log("Swipe Alto");
            }
            else
            {
                thisFrameTouchInput = DetectedTouchInput.SWIPE_DOWN;
                Debug.Log("Swipe Basso");
            }
        }
    }

    🤠 Predictive collision and safe rotation

    🧭 CONTEXT!

    The difficulty is that a tetromino is not a point. Each piece is made up of four blocks and everyone must stay inside the camp and out of the cells busy. Controlling only the pivot, obviously, was not enough. I have then reasoned about the immediate future of the piece: first I imagine the displacement of each block, then I decide whether to apply it, a predictive collision that I allows you to solve the problem before it even arises, avoiding it a priori overlap and collisions, but communicating them efficiently to the systems that are they occupy them.

    ❓ WHY?

    I wanted a command to always produce an understandable result: the piece moves or stays where it is. In particular, a rotation close to a wall or blocks already fixed must not create interpenetrations, this applies yes for movement and rotation. To achieve this coherence, the validation must occur before modifying the final state.

    🛠️ HOW?

    CheckTetraminoCollisionOnGrid iterates over the blocks of the tetramino, calculate the predicted cell by adding the input translation and check two conditions: exit from the horizontal or lower limits and presence of a value TETRAMIN in the grill. The cost is constant in practice, because one piece standard has a maximum of four blocks.

    MoveActivePiece controls the X axis and Y axis separately: if one direction is blocked, the other is not canceled by mistake.

    For rotations I store the Quaternion of the tetromino pivot, using the conversion functions, then, I apply 90 degrees and perform a predictive collision control. If a collision is detected, recovery the previous rotation; otherwise I fire the positioning event of active tetromino, to which services such as are registered "TetraminoAudioManager" or "TetraminoAnimationManager" for name two. This mechanism solves the problem of illegitimate rotations without introducing an external table of poses.

    Rotation algorithm 
    void RotateLeft()
{
    Quaternion originalRot =
        activePiece.rotationPivot.transform.localRotation;

    activePiece.rotationPivot.transform.localRotation =
        Quaternion.Euler
        (
            activePiece.rotationPivot.transform.localEulerAngles.x,
            activePiece.rotationPivot.transform.localEulerAngles.y,
            activePiece.rotationPivot.transform.localEulerAngles.z
            - 90f
        );

    bool hasCollided =
            CheckTetraminoCollisionOnGrid(activePiece, 0, 0).generalCollision;

    if (hasCollided)
    {
        activePiece.rotationPivot.transform.localRotation =
            originalRot;
    }
    else
    {
        onPieceRotate.Invoke();
    }
}

    🤰 Generation of tetrominoes and continuity of game

    🧭 CONTEXT!

    Now it was time to create the tetris "pouch", this algorithm was the precursor of controlled randomness in the game design, understanding how it worked wasn't just necessary to optimize the gameplay of this project, also because it is a very used stratagem in the field of game design. At the same time, I needed to know the piece next to update the preview icon.

    ❓ WHY?

    The algorithm called "pouch algorithm" is useful for not entrusting the extraction of each piece to a non-deterministic mechanism. A coincidence pure can repeat the same type for a long time and leave the player without one useful form: it is formally casual but perceived as unfair and for a long time go frustrating. I preferred a random order that keeps a controlled distribution, which is why this was designed algorithm.

    🛠️ HOW?

    The function CreateNewPiece constructs a list by mixing the seven tetromino prefabs with OrderBy to a random value. Adds a random eighth item as a bridge to the next pouch, generated separately from the other 7. When all 7 pieces come used, the 8 piece is used as a reference to reassemble the six more tetrominoes and prepare the bridge element again. Like this each cycle of seven pieces maintains the complete variety, while the queue remains ready for preview in UI.

    Aurto gutoda gnata: 100 🔥.

    CreateNewPiece instantiates the expected prefab, puts it under the piece wrapper, immediately check if the spawn collides with a block already fixed and, in that case, activates game over. If the spawn is valid, emit the event that updates the UI of the next tetromino. Generation is therefore connected to the match loop but does not mix with the interface.

    ♟️ Architectural patterns and strategies

    Lightweight singleton for shared services

    🧭 CONTEXT!

    Some services must be available from multiple points in the game without manually pass each reference in the object chain. The coroutines of utilities, touch status and loading some prefabs are examples of resources transversal to the single screen or to the single component.

    ❓ WHY?

    For these cases I have chosen an essential version of the Singleton, fast and simple to implement, sufficient to avoid repeated searches in the hierarchy and to make immediate access to a service already present in the scene. It's a limited choice: the heart of the game continues to receive many dependencies on the Inspector, instead of turning everything into state global.

    🛠️ HOW?

    TouchManager and PowUtilityU, for example maintain a static variable inst. Awake checks whether an instance already exists and stores if not the current one; the static Get method returns the service or evokes a error if it is not available.

    🐛 Encapsulation and serialized configuration

    🧭 CONTEXT!

    Many game variables must be adjustable during the balance: falling speed, swipe threshold, screen size grid, scores, clips and object references. Leave them all public would make these parameters error-prone in a business or context collaboration.

    ❓ WHY?

    I wanted to maintain the accessibility of the Inspector without giving up the state protection. This is why the data is thick serialized but exposed to the rest of the code with write properties private.

    🛠️ HOW?

    The code uses both private fields annotated with SerializeField and auto property with field SerializeField and private set. For example, parameters of the grid and the AudioSources can be set from the scene, while other components can read them but not freely assign them. It's a use of encapsulation contextualized to Unity's unique attributes.

    🪵 Game-typed data model

    🧭 CONTEXT!

    The grid logic needs to represent: different types of tiles, so as to be able to describe different behaviors; pieces available; lines candidates and references to the objects to be modified. Beyond the grid, I wanted it was possible to know the type of piece available, without having to create separate classes, since I would only have overcomplicated it.

    In essence, I had to store different information from each other and it was necessary for each of them to represent the correct state, or in the case of tiles, their typology.

    ❓ WHY?

    I chose strongly typed structures because the Tetris domain is naturally discreet. A tile can take on a few types known a priori e a piece belongs to a closed set of shapes that it can take. This makes conditions more readable and allows the compiler to highlight invalid uses, reducing the possibility of errors on the part of the reader and writes, as if I had used strings to get the shape of the piece.

    🛠️ HOW?

    I simply exploited the "enum" structure, for example, GridMapValues and TetraminoTypes are enums that they represent the state of a tile and the shape of the piece respectively. Then, moving in the context of the inputs, DetectedTouchInput carries out the same role for touch controls. Two-dimensional arrays are based on enums and memorize the grid and the visual references.

    ⌛ Management of time and delayed actions

    🧭 CONTEXT

    To manage non-temporally ordered action flows I created a Library that takes advantage of Unity's coroutine system. So, if for example, after deleting a line I had to wait 0.5 seconds before generate a new chunk, rather than saturate the code with coroutines, I have exploited anonymous functions that can be constructed on the fly.

    🛠️ HOW?

    PowUtilityU exposes functions that allow you to delay execution of a function, such as DelayAction, DoActionUntil, and RepeatActionForFrame. They get Action or Func and boot IEnumerators that return WaitForSeconds or null to the Unity engine. Additionally, the unique ID of the coroutine is stored for manipulation following its use. Thanks to Unity's coroutine system I call the same function as coroutine, the IDs will always be different, in this way conflicts cannot be created between coroutines with the same one behaviour.

    ❓ WHY?

    The need to delay some actions as a consequence of others is ubiquitous in GameDev, especially in event-driven architectures. Delegates and coroutines allow you to pass the behavior to be performed and to distribute it over time without creating additional threads or interrupting the current frame or without additional overhead. United with the architecture of PowUtilityU developed by me, I can exploit its potential without the need to create boilerplate code.

    💀 Post-mortem

    👥 TetrisLogic does not respect the Single-Responsibility

    Context

    TetrisLogic manages too many different logics, each of which can be decoupled with relative ease. For reasons related to time, as a deadline to meet, I had to streamline the period dedicated to design architectural.

    Error

    I turned TetrisLogic into a core class with too many responsibility. This type of class is convenient in the early stages, because that's enough open a file to find almost any rule; modify certain parts of code, however, becomes much more tedious as there are a myriad of rules closely linked to each other. A fix to the fall speed, for example example, and here I am navigating between lines dedicated to input, collision and to the management of the grid lines, in practice debugging becomes hell.

    How would I improve it

    I would separate the system into objects with clear tasks:

    • Board: grid status, cell check e deletion of lines;
    • PieceGenerator: bag of seven pieces and preview;
    • GameSession: game status and transitions between spawns, falling, clearing, pausing and game over;

    The Unity class which today is TetrisLogic would have the role as an orchestrator, he would provide events to sign up for and invoke them when useful by notifying each registered system, not to contain each implementation detail. Following precisely the principle of the Event driven design.

    🤔 The game states are not exhaustive and are not explicit

    Context

    Deleting a row requires a sequence: detect rows full, animate the blocks, delete them, lower the rest and generate a new piece, everything is tied to routines followed by functions, whether or not it is clear not reading the code line by line. Basically the routines of a match are managed by specific functions, nested and hidden between lines of code, rather than by solid classes and structures capable of describing them in a way the behavior is explicit, also for this reason it is not possible to trace the behavior match status in real-time, you can only register for the event corresponding that represents a "pseudo-state" of the game. This isn't necessarily a problem, as the game's limitations were well defined before the code was built, this system does not represents a limit when compared to the achievable size of the project, but given that the post-mortem exists to identify the points from improve to prepare myself for a corporate context, then yes that represents a problem.

    Error

    The mechanism works, but the limitation is that the state of the game is inferred from indirect variables like activePiece == null, hasGameOver and Time.timeScale. This makes it difficult to know what stage the game is actually in and increases the risk of unexpected transitions, especially with retries, pauses or changes future animations.

    How I would improve it

    I would introduce an explicit state machine:

    Spawning -> Falling -> Locking -> Clearing -> Spawning
                       |              |
                       v              v
                    GameOver        Paused

    Each state would be deducible in real-time and I would divide the logic, avoiding the ability to execute commands inconsistent with the context of the state of which they are part. Tracing an error in a certain state of the game would be much simpler thanks to the explicit structure and also the coupling reduced between states.

    🤔 I used UnityEvent and Singleton as shortcuts without set clearer boundaries

    Context

    I used UnityEvent to connect gameplay, UI, audio and animations directly in the Inspector. I also introduced Singleton lightweight for TouchManager, PowUtilityU, so as to retrieve shared services with a static method Get().

    Error

    These choices allowed me to work quickly in the scene, but some dependencies have become less visible in the code. With events configured in the Inspector I can forget a listener or connect a method with parameters that are no longer consistent without realizing it during one code review only. With Singletons, however, I make services available globals and I have to carefully manage lifecycle, initialization order and duplicates. I don't consider these tools to be wrong: the mistake is not having them consistently decided when to use them and when to prefer an addiction explicit.

    How would I improve it

    I would keep UnityEvent for the feedback link audiovisual, but I would pass the fundamental events of the domain through explicit C# interfaces or events. For example, GameSession could expose LineCleared and GameOver, while a presenter Unity subscribes to them and turns them into UI and animations. The goal is to reduce the number of Singletons, then I would create a single-entry-point for manage dependencies when initializing a game scene, while generic tools would become small, testable services.

    💠 I left some configurations distributed and fragile

    Context

    Scores, speed, prefab, icon sprites, audio clips and references scene are set in different components. Some operations load resources with strings, while switching between scenes uses written names directly in the code.

    Error

    The configuration in the Inspector is useful, but I left data linked between them at different points. Add a new tetromino or change the name of one scene requires remembering all the points to update: generators, icons, colors, prefabs and uploads. This increases the risk of a project that works in editor but fails after a reorganization of assets.

    How would I improve it

    I would create one or more ScriptableObject configuration. Every tetromino would have type, prefab, icon, possible color and rotation data; a match setup would contain scores and speed. For the scenes I would use references or a centralized catalog, avoiding sparse strings. In this way adding content would become a data modification, not one hunt for serialized switches and references.

    🧪 I neglected testing and used a fragile logging

    Context

    The cascading consequences of previous errors crystallize in difficulty testing the code. Not only that, but most of the control was done by testing the scene and printing many Debug.Log. For a visual prototype is a natural way to proceed, but the grid contains discrete rules that lend themselves very well to automatic testing. Furthermore, a Debug.Log only works in the short term, in complex systems it is necessary to have to store a lot of state information and be able to filter based on the context of the problem you are trying to solve. In practice, you need a logging system that is much more robust than a simple one Debug.Log.

    Error

    • I have not isolated the modules enough from Unity to test them properly fast. This makes it more difficult to demonstrate that collision, rotation, multiple deletion and generation always respect the same invariants, for example.
    • I have used non-exhaustive logging tools. Like, I could have exploit the logging package.

    How would I improve it

    I would write test edit mode on the pure domain, even before the play modes test:

    • a piece cannot exit from the left, right or bottom;
    • a rotation cannot overlap with occupied cells;
    • a solid row is removed and the top rows go down correctly;
    • two or more lines deleted in the same shift are compacted in the right order;
    • each bag contains each of the seven types once;
    • the preview coincides with the next piece extracted.

    I would then add explicit development controls in the model, such as consistent matrix sizes and always valid cells. In parallel, I would have used the logging package.
